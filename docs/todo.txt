1. [OPEN, HIGH VALUE, cheap] Add 1/sqrt(d) scaling to the bilinear
   adjacency logits before ReLU/softmax. main.tex line ~209 already
   states this is missing and flags it as relevant to the collapse.
   Standard scaled-attention practice; nobody has actually tried it yet.
   File: src/models/vmd_mfgnn.py, FrequencyGraphConstructor. Cheap to
   test in isolation from every other change.

2. [OPEN, MODERATE CONFIDENCE] Try Softplus instead of LeakyReLU in the
   GATv2 layer. Neumeier et al. (IEEE IV 2023, arXiv:2305.16196)
   independently found GATv2 gradients vanishing to exactly zero on
   small, sparse graphs — the same symptom measured directly in this
   project's own checkpoints (gradient to the graph embeddings hits
   precisely 0.0 at convergence, main.tex sec:graphfix). One paper, a
   different domain (automotive), but the mechanism match is real.

3. [OPEN] Re-test AdamW narrowly. Adam+L2 and AdamW are provably
   non-equivalent specifically for parameters with small, sparse
   gradient history — exactly what the graph embeddings are. The
   earlier "AdamW = no difference" finding this project used may not
   have isolated this specific low-gradient-history effect. Measure the
   embedding parameters' effective decay magnitude directly, not just
   final accuracy, before trusting the old conclusion either way.

4. [OPEN] Search weight_decay itself via HPO instead of only the
   binary graphfix on/off. The whole collapse story traces to
   weight_decay=1e-5 applied uniformly. HPO already has code support
   for searching weight_decay (search_weight_decay=True in
   src/hpo.py) but the real reported run never used it. A log-uniform
   search over e.g. 1e-6 to 1e-3 could find a middle ground — enough
   decay to regularize, not enough to erase the graph — without needing
   the full graphfix intervention (which is known to hurt accuracy).

5. [OPEN] Isolate what's actually causing graphfix/graphfix_temp to
   underperform. The fix (unit-normalize embeddings + exclude from
   weight decay) genuinely stops the collapse — real, non-uniform
   structure confirmed directly in the trained weights — but both
   variants score worse than the collapsed full_model. Two changes are
   bundled together; nobody has tested them separately. Run two more
   ablation variants: normalization alone (decay still on), and
   no-decay alone (no normalization), to find out which change is
   responsible, or whether it's neither and the real cause is something
   else (e.g. these variants being undertrained relative to their
   different parameterization).

6. [OPEN] Re-run the exact-zero-gradient diagnostic on the NEW N=15
   graphfix variants. The "gradient decays to exactly 0.0 independent
   of weight decay" finding (main.tex sec:graphfix, an unexplained
   second collapse mechanism) is from the OLDER N=8-era graph-fix
   experiment, not yet re-measured on full_model_graphfix /
   full_model_graphfix_temp from this session's real N=15 run. Confirm
   it still holds before leaning on it for anything further.

7. [OPEN, BIGGER SCOPE] Try Gumbel-softmax / L0-regularized structure
   learning instead of discrete top-k sparsification. Builds sparsity
   into the training objective directly, rather than masking a softmax
   after the fact — a structurally different mechanism than what's been
   tried so far. Most of the literature behind this is tested at
   hundreds/thousands of nodes, not N=15 — a real hypothesis, not a
   proven transfer. Bigger lift than items 1-6; do those first.

8. [OPEN, MULTI-SEED VALIDATION NEEDED] Several of this session's real
   findings are single-run and need replication before being treated as
   stable: the null-control experiment's h=5/h=10 DM significance
   (real input scoring worse than shuffled/noise input), and the HPO
   winner's ~12% edge over the trial cluster (could be seed luck, zero
   replication currently). Re-run the null-control experiment and HPO
   at 3-5 seeds each before citing either result as settled.

13. [WORTH CONSIDERING, BIGGER SCOPE, NOT A FIX] Reframe the prediction
    target as volatility instead of price-level return. RQ0 already
    shows nothing beats a naive price forecast on this data. A related-

    citable precedent that volatility may be a more forecastable target
    than returns for this asset class. This does not fix the graph
    collapse finding; it's a separate question about whether the whole
    task is framed at a point where deep learning has a real shot.
