# Section 3 — Decomposition methods (VMD, EMD, EEMD, CEEMDAN, EWT, SSA, wavelet) combined with deep learning

The direct methodological lineage of VMD-MFGNN. This is the largest section, and it splits into two groups: (a) the mainstream "decompose → forecast each mode → sum" ensemble literature, which reports large accuracy gains, and (b) a smaller but decisive group (Niyogi; Zhang et al., FSDB; Feng et al., VMDNet; Li et al., sliding-window VMD) showing that those gains are substantially manufactured by **decomposing the full series before splitting**, which leaks future information into the training modes. Group (b) is the direct justification for this project's rolling-window VMD refit.

# Section 4 — Graph Neural Networks for financial/commodity time series, and GNN failure modes

The largest section, and the one that carries this project's central negative finding. It divides into three groups: (a) **foundational architectures** whose components VMD-MFGNN uses directly (GAT, GATv2, MTGNN); (b) **failure-mode theory** — over-smoothing, over-squashing, rank collapse, spectral failure, static attention — which supplies the vocabulary and prior art for the observed graph-embedding collapse; and (c) **financial/commodity GNN applications**, including two papers (Niu et al.; Gregnanin et al.) that independently question whether learned financial graphs carry real signal at all.

| **Point (Deterministic) / Interval (Probabilistic) Forecasting** | **Not applicable**. |

The critical experiment (E1) demonstrates the two corrections are **not substitutes**: a **deliberately leaky oracle posting a Sharpe ratio of 35 survives Deflated Sharpe and probability-of-backtest-overfitting testing completely**. Evaluation covers a 453-stock point-in-time US equity universe and a 39-ETF multi-asset universe with realistic transaction, impact and borrow costs, two frontier LLMs, search budgets up to one hundred candidates, five repeated runs, stationary-bootstrap confidence intervals, and a human trader's production rule system assessed under identical instruments. The framework also formalizes why **pre-registered hypotheses earn lower evidential bars than brute search**, and quantifies the sample sizes credible certification of moderate edges actually requires. |
| **Model** | An LLM-driven strategy-discovery system with structural leakage guardrails and search-aware deflation; arms include two frontier LLMs, passive benchmarks, a leaky oracle control, and a human trader's production rules. |
| **Method** | Multivariate (453-stock point-in-time equity universe; 39-ETF multi-asset universe). |
| **Single-Step-Ahead / Multi-Step-Ahead (which kind)** | Strategy-level evaluation over design and evaluation windows rather than fixed-horizon forecasting. |
| **Point (Deterministic) / Interval (Probabilistic) Forecasting** | Deterministic strategy rules, evaluated with **stationary-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals** on out-of-sample Sharpe. |
| **Merits** | Delivers the sharpest single result in this section, and one that changes how leakage should be argued: **statistical correction does not catch leakage.** A planted look-ahead oracle with Sharpe 35 passes every deflation and overfitting test — so reporting a Deflated Sharpe, a PBO, or a significance test is *not* evidence that a protocol is leakage-free, and only a structural guardrail on the information set can provide that. This is the strongest available justification for describing a leakage-safe protocol at the level of construction (as this project does with its rolling VMD refit) rather than relying on downstream statistics. The **evaporation curve** is a directly adoptable diagnostic — best-of-first-k in-sample Sharpe climbing from **−0.07 to 1.69 over 102 self-recorded trials while the deflation threshold climbs faster** is a vivid, quantified picture of search inflation, and applies equally to a human researcher iterating over model variants. The headline outcome is a clean, well-controlled negative: honest evaluation **certifies passive benchmarks** (out-of-sample CIs excluding zero) and **rejects every LLM-discovered strategy** across two models, search budgets to one hundred candidates, and five repeated runs. The author's care with interpretation is exemplary — "a CI spanning zero is not evidence of absence". The observation that this applies to **any agentic system that self-reports its best result** generalises the point beyond finance. |
| **Drawbacks** | The domain is LLM-driven strategy discovery, where trial counts are inflated by agent autonomy in a way that has no direct analogue in a study fitting a small number of pre-specified model variants — so the magnitude of the search-inflation correction does not transfer, even though the principle does. Equities and ETFs, not commodities. Single independent researcher, arXiv preprint, no peer review. The negative result is about LLM-discovered strategies under this particular system and search budget, not about LLM strategy discovery in general. Deflation requires a complete trial ledger, which is only possible when the search is instrumented from the outset — a retrofit is not available. |
| **Dataset** | A **453-stock point-in-time US equity universe** (PIT top-200 used in E1; design window 2017–2021, evaluation window 2022–2025) and a **39-ETF multi-asset universe**, both with realistic transaction, impact and borrow costs. Search budgets up to 100 candidates across two frontier LLMs with five repeated runs; a trial ledger of 102 self-recorded trials in E3. |
| **Accuracy Measure** | **Deflated Sharpe Ratio indexed to the recorded search**, **probabilistic (undeflated) Sharpe**, probability of backtest overfitting, and **evaluation-window Sharpe with stationary-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals**. Reported: a planted look-ahead oracle with **Sharpe 35** survives statistical deflation completely (Table 2, E1); the evaporation curve shows best in-sample Sharpe climbing from **−0.07 to 1.69** over 102 trials while the deflation threshold climbs faster (Figure 4); **only equal-weight buy-and-hold clears the 0.95 deflated threshold** (Figure 2); and **only the passive benchmarks have evaluation-window confidence intervals excluding zero** (Figure 5), with every LLM-discovered strategy rejected. |

## A.3 Cross-cutting findings the corpus supports

Five claims are supported by multiple independent entries and are therefore safe to make in `main.tex` with more than one citation:

1. **Simple baselines frequently match or beat elaborate architectures.** Zeng et al. (2.3, linear beats five Transformers in all cases); Ly et al. (2.1, LSTM does not systematically beat ARIMA); Muhammad et al. (2.10, naive persistence dominates on near-random-walk commodities); Wang & Zhang (2.9, from-scratch deep models rank 10th–16th of 17); Wang & Lu (1.1, HAR beats deep learning on daily copper realized volatility); Baquero (5.8, no peer-reviewed Bitcoin study robustly beats the naive baseline); Gençay (5.11, only passive benchmarks survive honest evaluation); Zhang et al. (3.8, naive XGBoost beats the decomposition hybrid at one of three stations).

2. **One-time decomposition before splitting leaks future information and inflates results.** Niyogi (3.7, mechanism plus the Moving Front remedy); Zhang et al. (3.8, FSDB sampling, quantified); Feng et al. (3.16, VMDNet — and the further point that decomposing *after* the split is still insufficient); Li (3.14, sliding-window VMD); Zhang & Duan (2.6) and Boadi (3.18) as documented instances of the uncorrected pattern; Zhang et al. (5.7) identifying centered temporal features as one of the two dominant leakage channels.

3. **GNN representations degenerate for reasons that are spectral and weight-dependent, not merely depth-dependent.** Oono & Suzuki (4.2); Cai & Wang (4.5, Dirichlet energy); Roth & Liebig (4.12, rank collapse, independent of learned transformations); Rusch et al. (4.10, axiomatic definition and measures); Roth et al. (4.15, and the crucial rider that fixing collapse does not guarantee a better model).

4. **Graph attention has a characterised regime in which it provably cannot recover structure.** Fountoulakis et al. (4.8, the hard regime); Brody et al. (4.7, GAT's static attention and its noise-sensitivity); Priebe et al. (4.6, approximately regular graphs with signal off the leading eigenvector); Kumar et al. (4.17, an eight-node global-index graph with pairwise correlations above 0.90 — an empirical instance of near-regularity); Wu et al. (4.3, on-par performance exactly where real structure already exists).

5. **Directional accuracy and headline metrics are uninterpretable without an honest baseline and a dispersion check.** Cheung (5.9, the base-rate artifact demonstrated and quantified); Baquero (5.8, the task-to-baseline taxonomy); Kapoor & Narayanan (5.2, metric-choice issues and cut-off selection on test data); Jiang et al. (4.18) and You et al. (4.14) as instances of stock-trend classification reported in the 52–65% regime without a majority-class baseline.

## A.4 Genuine gaps in this corpus

These are stated explicitly so that no field of this review is read as more complete than it is.

- **Copper-specific deep learning is almost absent from arXiv.** The most topically central copper papers — Liu et al. (2020) VMD-LSTM for non-ferrous metals, Sun et al. (2025) multi-view graph transformer, Li & Liu (2026) MBTI-Net (complexity-aware VMD + GNN on copper futures) — are journal-only and appear in Section 1A **without content verification**. The two closest published analogues to VMD-MFGNN are both in that unverified list. Anyone extending this review should obtain those PDFs through a subscription and complete their twelve fields properly.
- **Weak-form efficiency testing on LME/COMEX copper.** No entry tests variance-ratio or random-walk hypotheses on copper specifically. Brouty & Garcin (5.4) and Takaishi (5.5) are the closest available substitutes and neither covers copper or uses a variance-ratio test; Takaishi covers Gold only. This gap was flagged in `bibliography_index.md` and remains open.
- **Per-frequency-band graph learning on a small-N financial panel.** The two strongest precedents for the architecture — Ahmad et al. VMGCN (3.11) and MAGN (3.15) — operate on traffic graphs of thousands of sensors. No entry in this corpus demonstrates the mechanism at N ≈ 8 on financial data, which is precisely the regime this project occupies and precisely the regime the theory in 4.6 and 4.8 identifies as difficult.
- **Probabilistic / interval forecasting.** Barely represented. Entry **1.4** (Bastianin et al.) is the only one that explicitly targets *point and density* forecasts; **5.11** (Gençay) reports stationary-bootstrap confidence intervals around a deterministic strategy's out-of-sample Sharpe; and **2.4** (Zhang et al.) merely *names* point-to-interval extension as an open research direction without doing it. The applied decomposition and graph literatures in Sections 3 and 4 are entirely deterministic. (The four entries tallied as "Both" in A.2 are counted on a different basis — they are the entries whose forecast-type field spans two categories, which for the two surveys among them, 4.13 and 5.8, means the *reviewed corpus* covers both, not that the paper itself produces density forecasts.)
- **Seed and initialisation stability.** Only a minority of entries report multi-seed results with dispersion (3.16 Feng et al., five fixed seeds; 4.1 Veličković et al., 100 runs; 4.3 Wu et al., 10 runs; 4.7 Brody et al., 4.12 Roth & Liebig, 4.14 You et al., 5.1 Wasserbacher & Spindler, 5.9 Cheung). The rest report single-run numbers, which is a systemic weakness of the corpus rather than of any one paper.
